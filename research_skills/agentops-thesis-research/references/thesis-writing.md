# Thesis Writing Workflow

Use this when drafting or revising thesis prose.

## Build The Argument First

Before writing a section, state:

- section role in the thesis;
- one-sentence section claim;
- 3 to 7 paragraph roles;
- evidence needed for each role;
- unresolved inputs.

## Chapter-Level Guidance

Likely thesis structure:

1. Introduction: problem, motivation, research question, contributions.
2. Background: LLM agents, multi-agent systems, AgentOps/observability concepts.
3. Related Work: monitoring, tracing, failure attribution, multi-agent debugging.
4. Method: proposed taxonomy/tool/framework/analysis pipeline.
5. Experiments or Case Study: tasks, traces, failures, metrics, analysis.
6. Discussion: limitations, threats to validity, future work.
7. Conclusion: concise answer to the research question.

## Paragraph Rules

- One paragraph should carry one message.
- The first sentence should reveal the paragraph's role.
- Connect sentences by cause, contrast, consequence, or refinement.
- Keep terms stable: failure, error, anomaly, root cause, attribution, trace, span, event, action, agent.
- Weaken or remove claims that lack evidence.

## Revision Pass

For each section, run:

1. Reverse outline: list paragraph topic sentences.
2. Claim-evidence check: mark each important claim as supported, weak, or unsupported.
3. Flow check: identify jumps, repeated claims, and missing transitions.
4. Terminology check: ensure the same concept is named consistently.
5. Reader check: identify what a skeptical thesis reviewer would question.

## Output Contract

When drafting or revising, provide:

- compact outline;
- revised text;
- claim-evidence notes;
- unresolved inputs;
- next writing gate.
