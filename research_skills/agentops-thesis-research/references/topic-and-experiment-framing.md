# Topic And Experiment Framing

Use this when shaping the thesis direction, research question, contributions, or experiments.

## Research Question Gate

Before committing to a direction, write:

- `RQ`: one precise research question;
- `Object`: what system behavior is studied;
- `Intervention`: what method, tool, or framework is proposed;
- `Measurement`: what evidence would show it works;
- `Baseline`: what it is compared against;
- `Boundary`: where the claim does not apply.

## Contribution Shapes

For this repository, likely contribution types are:

1. `Taxonomy`: classify multi-agent failures or observability needs.
2. `Instrumentation`: collect structured traces from agent workflows.
3. `Attribution`: identify which agent/action/message caused failure.
4. `Benchmark`: evaluate monitoring or attribution across tasks.
5. `Tooling`: build a practical AgentOps workflow for diagnosis.
6. `Empirical study`: analyze failures in existing multi-agent systems.

Prefer a thesis direction that can show both conceptual clarity and demonstrable evidence.

## Experiment Planning

For each proposed experiment, record:

- hypothesis;
- dataset or task suite;
- agents/tools involved;
- failure injection or natural failure source;
- trace signals collected;
- attribution target;
- metric;
- baseline;
- expected threat to validity.

Do not present an experiment as completed until results exist in files, logs, notebooks, or code output.

## Decision Matrix

When comparing directions, score each candidate from 1 to 5:

| Criterion | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Novelty | Is the contribution distinct from existing papers? |
| Feasibility | Can it be completed within thesis time and available tools? |
| Evidence | Can it produce measurable or inspectable support? |
| Thesis fit | Does it align with the user's existing paper collection and notes? |
| Risk | Lower score means high risk; explain why. |

Recommend the highest overall direction only after naming its biggest risk.
